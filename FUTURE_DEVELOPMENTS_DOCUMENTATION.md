A possible improvement on this line of research may include the introduction of populations of models. 
What we mean by populations of models is having different versions of the same class of model (e.g. more than one VGG19), which differ in terms of training in different possible ways. 
- The first way we thought of revolves around the stochasticity of training, meaning that even if the hyperparameters and dataset are the exact same between different training runs, the final model that the training converges to will very hardly be the same, because of the stochasticity in choosing which images (samples) to use for a specific training batch. This kind of population is the most interesting one in my opinion, because the models of such a population have fixed common characteristics in terms of training parameters
- The second one could be to just consider the same model class, trained with different hyperparameters. The hyperparameters in question would mostly revolve around this task's specific parameters, so for example the occlusion ratio.
- A combination of the two is also possible

What we could do with these populations is we could compute cumulative saliency maps, like we do with humans, so we could for example obtain a total aggregated saliency map for a family of models (e.g. all VGG19 models), or the best performing tail, or whatever else. A cumulative map could also be useful for a more stable saliency result. Other analysis could resolve around the machine domain itself, so analyzing if different models of the same family yield different saliency results and try and find reasons for that. There are countless analyses that could be performed.
